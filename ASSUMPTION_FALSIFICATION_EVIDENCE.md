# Assumption Falsification Evidence — Enterprise Coordination Failure Tax

**Date:** 2026-07-27  
**Mode:** Truth-first, disconfirming-evidence prioritized  
**Status:** Draft v1 (web-evidence synthesis)

---

## 1) Scope and method

This dossier stress-tests the frozen strategy around the core hypothesis:

> Organizations lose large enterprise value because local teams optimize local KPIs, and this coordination failure is persistent enough to justify a new category-level product.

For each assumption, we capture:
- supporting evidence,
- contradictory evidence,
- implication,
- confidence score (0–100),
- action recommendation.

---

## 2) Evidence quality notes (important)

- **High confidence sources in this pass:** Gartner and PwC public research/news pages with explicit survey/sample details.
- **Medium confidence:** PMI public report pages (summary-level percentages).
- **Lower confidence / directional only:** commercial market-sizing pages with internal inconsistencies.
- **Access failures:** some McKinsey, Deloitte, Bain, IDC pages were blocked/redirected/404 or non-extractable.

## 3) Assumption evidence matrix

## A1. The problem is widespread in large enterprises (not niche)

**Assumption:** Decision rework and late-stage reversals are common enough to support broad demand.

**Supporting evidence**
- Gartner supply-chain leadership survey reports **72%** revisited final approval decisions at least once, and over half revisited 3+ times (sample details published: 151 leaders, Nov–Dec 2025, firms ≥$250M revenue).
- Gartner framing links rework to turbulence/volatility and margin pressure.

**Contradictory evidence**
- Evidence is currently strongest in supply chain and finance-adjacent contexts; direct cross-function proof across all C-suite domains is still incomplete.

**Implication**
- Strong signal that decision friction is real; category breadth remains partially unproven until multi-function data is added.

**Confidence:** 82/100  
**Action:** Proceed, but collect additional function-specific evidence (HR, commercial, operations) before hard horizontal claims.

---

## A2. Enterprises are under-investing in decision quality relative to productivity AI

**Assumption:** The market overweights automation/productivity and underweights judgment quality.

**Supporting evidence**
- Gartner finance survey: **45%** of AI investments prioritize productivity while only **20%** prioritize decision quality.
- Board expectation gap noted in the same Gartner release context.

**Contradictory evidence**
- Could be temporary sequencing (enterprises may start with productivity and move to decision quality later).

**Implication**
- Clear positioning opportunity: “decision quality and economic coherence” as differentiated wedge.

**Confidence:** 84/100  
**Action:** Keep category language anchored on decision quality, but include migration path from productivity budgets.

---

## A3. Executive urgency exists for value leakage and execution risk

**Assumption:** CEOs/investors perceive material execution and value-realization risk now.

**Supporting evidence**
- PwC CEO and investor survey pages show repeated signals on confidence pressure, AI value realization gaps, trust and disclosure expectations, and risk concentration.
- PMI summary indicates business-acumen distribution gaps (moderate/high/low stratification), supporting uneven decision capability.

**Contradictory evidence**
- Public survey summaries can over-index perception and under-specify operational causality.

**Implication**
- Narrative urgency is strong; causal linkage to coordination tax still needs customer-level operational casework.

**Confidence:** 76/100  
**Action:** Pair macro survey proof with 8–12 enterprise operational case studies.

---

## A4. This problem creates a defensible, compounding moat (years of learning)

**Assumption:** Organization-specific decision memory compounds and is hard to replicate quickly.

**Supporting evidence**
- AI 100 evidence suggests vertical winners with proprietary/rare workflow data and deep embedding show stronger lock-in dynamics.
- Observability/governance and “Know Your Agent” layers indicate enterprise appetite for accountable non-human actions in workflows.

**Contradictory evidence**
- Incumbents (ERP, cloud, data platforms) can ship adjacent capabilities rapidly if adoption economics are clear.
- Moat from data alone can weaken if standards/open connectors reduce switching friction.

**Implication**
- Moat is possible but not automatic; it must be designed around proprietary decision-outcome memory and workflow entrenchment.

**Confidence:** 68/100  
**Action:** Treat moat as execution-dependent; explicitly design proprietary memory schema and outcome benchmarking loops.

---

## A5. Competitive pressure is intensifying and capital is concentrated

**Assumption:** The startup path is harder because capital and attention concentrate in mega-round winners.

**Supporting evidence**
- CB Insights Venture Q2’26: **263 mega-rounds captured 81% of total funding**; deal count at decade+ lows.
- State of AI Q2’26: **89% of AI funding** went to 142 mega-rounds.

**Contradictory evidence**
- Concentration can still leave white-space for workflow-specific enterprise products if GTM is ROI-provable and wedge-focused.

**Implication**
- Generic horizontal AI positioning is structurally disadvantaged; tight wedge and hard ROI proof become mandatory.

**Confidence:** 88/100  
**Action:** Avoid broad “AI platform” framing; win one severe workflow and quantify payback aggressively.

---

## A6. Startup failure risk is real even with large funding

**Assumption:** Capital raised does not protect against PMF, timing, and unit-economics failures.

**Supporting evidence**
- CB Insights analysis of **431** VC-backed shutdowns since 2023: top cited final cause is running out of capital (**70%**), with major root causes including PMF, timing/macro, and economics.
- Total failed cohort equity in analysis: **$17.5B**; median time from last raise to death ~22 months.

**Contradictory evidence**
- Failure datasets can blend sector-specific dynamics; not all lessons transfer equally to enterprise workflow software.

**Implication**
- Strategy must include anti-failure controls: narrow wedge, shorter time-to-value, strong renewal economics, low-dependency GTM.

**Confidence:** 86/100  
**Action:** Build operating doctrine around failure-prevention metrics from day 1.

---

## A7. IP crowding exists in workflow/decision-support mechanics

**Assumption:** Parts of workflow orchestration and decision support are heavily patented/standardized.

**Supporting evidence**
- Patent search term set returns large corpus (tens of thousands in query context).
- Prior examples include historical enterprise decision-support and workflow/logging architectures.

**Contradictory evidence**
- Several cited patents are expired, domain-specific, or broad/legacy and do not necessarily block modern implementations.

**Implication**
- Novelty should not rely on generic workflow mechanics; differentiation must be in economic model, organizational memory, and outcomes protocol.

**Confidence:** 64/100  
**Action:** Add formal freedom-to-operate review before implementation phase.

---

## A8. TAM is large, but market-sizing evidence is noisy

**Assumption:** The adjacent BI/analytics market is large enough to support category creation.

**Supporting evidence**
- Multiple market reports point to multi-decade growth and large absolute spend.

**Contradictory evidence**
- Extracted market pages show internal inconsistencies (e.g., conflicting base-year/forecast values alongside identical CAGR), reducing evidentiary reliability.

**Implication**
- TAM is likely sufficient directionally, but current citations are not investment-grade.

**Confidence:** 52/100  
**Action:** Replace with auditable sources (10-K vendor disclosures, Gartner/IDC paid extracts, or primary bottoms-up model).

## 4) What this evidence says (brutal synthesis)

1. **Core pain is likely real and expensive** (strongest proof from Gartner decision rework data).  
2. **Market behavior validates your wedge** (decision quality underfunded vs productivity).  
3. **Execution risk is very high** due to funding concentration and failure patterns.  
4. **Moat is not guaranteed**; must be engineered through unique decision-outcome memory and workflow lock-in.  
5. **Current TAM citations are weak** and need stronger sourcing.

---

## 5) Kill criteria (if observed, stop or pivot)

- No measurable reduction in decision rework cycle within first lighthouse deployments.
- No quantified enterprise value lift attributable to cross-functional coordination changes.
- Buyer interest remains “innovation theater” with no budget migration from existing systems.
- Deployment needs heavy services to sustain usage (signals weak productization).

---

## 6) Survive-and-win criteria

- Proven reduction in approval revisits/rework for one mission-critical workflow.
- Documented annualized value impact at or above the £1M threshold in at least 2 lighthouse accounts.
- Renewal-led expansion from one function to two additional functions without major reimplementation.
- Evidence that replacing the system would forfeit organization-specific decision memory.
